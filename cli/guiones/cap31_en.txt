Torsion table in Hekatan Struct: the beam torsion depends on the slab mesh.
The table: a 10 cm Shell-Thin slab, four 30x50 beams and four 40x40 columns, pinned at the base.
I pick combination UDCon2 (1.2 Dead + 1.6 Live + 1.2 SDL) and the torsion diagram of the frames.
In elevation: the slab bends and rotates at its edge. At each shared node the slab rotation is the beam rotation, taken as torsion T = GJ dθ/dx. Next to the column the torsion is maximum.
Wilson, section 7.7, Fig. 7.6: slab and beam are tied by a rigid-body constraint, and compatibility may require applying it at several sections along the beam axis.
In section 8.9.5 he warns that plate elements must not model beam torsion. The frame carries the torsion with its GJ; the slab only imposes the rotation.
With a single slab element the beam shares only its ends: both rotate equally and the torsion is zero. ETABS with the same mesh also gives zero.
2x2 mesh: one shared node at midspan and the torsion rises to 2.62 tonf·m. ETABS gives 2.53.
4x4 mesh: five shared nodes and the torsion reaches 5.04.
8x8 mesh: 5.85.
16x16 mesh: 6.06. Between 8 and 16 it changes less than 4%: converged.
Full table: torsion grows with refinement, in Hekatan and in ETABS, and converges. A coarse mesh underestimates it.
If the beam is tied to the slab only at its ends, torsion is zero again and the slab edge is free: midspan deflection goes from 33 to 210 mm.
Half-beam equilibrium: dT/dx equals the slab edge moment. Integrated over half the span it gives the support torsion; at 32x32 it closes within 1%.
With the fine mesh the beam torsion is 6.06 tonf·m, above the cracking torsion of 2.50: the beam cracks. Cracked, its torsional stiffness drops on the order of 85 to 90%. The rotation the beam no longer restrains is taken by the slab: moment redistributes and panel equilibrium is preserved. This is compatibility torsion, which may be reduced; equilibrium torsion, needed to carry the load, may not. Reducing J and iterating is a secant approximation of the nonlinear behavior.
ACI 318 section 22.7.3.2: for compatibility torsion the design torsion may be reduced to φTcr = 1.88 tonf·m. The beam J is reduced until the torsion drops to that value.
In 3D, for reference: the slab colored by moment M22 with the uncracked beam. At the edge, -3.35 tonf·m/m; at the center, +2.26.
With J at 4%, torsion drops to 1.94. The edge moment drops to -0.87 and the center rises to 3.34. At the central section, slab plus beams plus frame thrust add up to 65.68 tonf·m in every case: moment redistributes, it does not vanish.
Consequence for reinforcement: the positive moment at the slab center rises 48%; bottom steel is designed with the moment after the beam cracks. The beam gets closed stirrups and longitudinal steel for φTcr.
Conclusion: beam torsion rises as the mesh is refined. The beam does not change; what changes is at how many nodes the slab imposes its rotation. A coarse mesh underestimates torsion.
