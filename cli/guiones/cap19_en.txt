A Warren truss in Hekatan Struct, start to finish, using only the quick-access ribbon.
I close both side panels. The ribbon has tabs, like AutoCAD: Draw holds drawing, supports, loads and modify.
I press Front, the X Z elevation, and turn on SNAP so every click lands on the grid. I zoom in with the mouse wheel.
Bottom chord: Polyline and seven clicks, from minus six to six metres, every two metres. Enter finishes.
Top chord: another polyline, six clicks at two metres high, over the centre of each panel.
Diagonals: one zigzag polyline, thirteen clicks. A click on an existing node reuses it. Thirteen nodes, twenty-three members.
Supports: I press Fixed and click each end. Next to it is Pinned, which leaves the rotations free.
Loads: minus ten kilonewtons in the box. I press Load and click each top node. The app solves by itself.
Distributed load: minus five kilonewtons per metre, Load q, and one click on each top chord segment.
Results tab: Deformed, scale times one thousand in its box, and Axial. Maximum deflection is zero point two nine eight millimetres.
Moment three-three, and the reactions at both supports.
2D diagram: the elevation with the diagram and its values, no perspective. I close it with the cross.
Back to the Draw tab. With Measure, support to support: twelve metres.
3D view and Zoom Extents. ETABS 22 gives the same deflection at all thirteen nodes.
Save As, at the top, asks for the name: cercha warren dot heks. It is plain text: nodes, members, supports and loads.
