We are going to replicate the real chapel, the one from the ETABS model, bar by bar and node by node, using only the cursor and the drawing menu, and I will say everything I do.
I prepare the canvas: I empty it, I turn off the grid because the real dimensions are not round, I turn off ortho mode, I turn off the auxiliary axis snap and polar tracking so the cursor does not stick to an axis when I mark a node, and I close both side panels with their sliding doors to draw with the whole canvas.
First axis, Y equal to eight point seven four. I set the work plane to the front elevation of that axis and with the Line tool I draw every bar of the model between its two nodes: the two columns, the three straight segments of the arch, which is already like that in ETABS because ETABS does not allow curves, the rafter, the ridge beam and the five segments of the wing arch down to the ground.
I do the same on the other axes: I change the work plane to each elevation, bring the camera closer, and draw its bars with Line, node by node. On axes twelve twenty-four and sixteen fifty-four there are also the buttress arches down to the ground, and the mezzanine beam appears only on some axes. When a click does not land within three centimetres of the node, I undo with control Z and repeat it with the camera closer.
Now the side planes: there go the longitudinal beams crossing the axes, again with Line between their nodes. They are fifty-seven bars: the purlins of the nave, those of the wing and those of the buttresses.
Finally the mezzanine diagonals, snapping with osnap to nodes already drawn. And a note: that mezzanine, hanging up next to the ridge, is an error in the ETABS model that the IFC reveals; here I replicate it as is to compare, and then we lower it.
I set the work plane back to the plan view, and this is the EDB chapel replicated: I drag with the mouse along the bottom of the canvas and we orbit to see it complete.
