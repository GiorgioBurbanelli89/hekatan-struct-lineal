We are going to draw two curved surfaces in Hekatan Struct, from the menu and with the cursor: a dome and the skin of the Allianz Arena. Both come out as flat panels, which is what ETABS accepts, and both were checked against ETABS node by node.
New file. I press Plane X Z, a true orthographic elevation. In Precision I turn on grid snap, so every click lands exactly on the grid, and set eight segments per arc.
The meridian of the dome: I press Arc by three points and click three times: the springing at five zero, the middle point at three four, which is at radius five because three squared plus four squared is five squared, and the crown at zero five, right on the axis. The arc comes out in eight segments.
Now I select it with a window: press Select, one click at a corner, move the cursor and another click at the opposite corner, as in AutoCAD. The eight segments are selected.
In Areas I set sixteen sectors and press Revolve selection. A single click on the vertical axis, and the meridian spins: every node of the arc becomes a ring of sixteen nodes, and the panels appear between rings.
Isometric view. One hundred twenty panels and one hundred twenty-nine nodes, all on the sphere of radius five with an error of ten to the minus thirteen. Look at the pole from above: the node on the axis cannot give quadrilaterals, so the cap is closed with kites, convex four-node panels, no collapsed triangles. Against ETABS, the deflection is zero point zero forty millimetres in both programs.
Now the Allianz, at one to ten scale. I clear everything and switch to plan. Four segments per corner, a five metre fillet, and Slab with fillets: two clicks at the corners, minus thirteen minus twelve and thirteen twelve, and the twenty-six by twenty-four outline with rounded corners appears.
Back to the elevation, I draw the bulge with Parabola by three points: at the foot sixteen zero, at mid height it bulges one metre, seventeen two, and at the top it returns to sixteen four. Twelve segments in height. I draw it away from the outline, because in the elevation all its vertices project onto the same pixel and the snap would grab any of them.
A single window selects the outline and the profile. I press Sweep in elevation and click once at the centre of the plan: at every height of the profile, the outline moves outward by the bulge, and the panels appear between rings. Four hundred eighty panels and five hundred twenty nodes.
In isometric you see the skin of the Allianz: the rounded plan and the bulge in height. The rings are exact, with an error of ten to the minus fifteen, and against ETABS the deflection is one point sixty-three millimetres in both programs, worst node at zero point zero eleven percent. The revolution is the same sweep with a circular outline.
